What the sofalog app does with your data

Effective 24 November 2026 · applies to the sofalog app for Android

sofalog is a tracker for the movies and shows you watch. The Android app keeps your library on your phone. There is no sofalog account, and the developer of sofalog runs no server for the app and never receives, sees or stores anything you do in it. This page describes where your data is instead: your device, and the few outside services the app contacts to show you titles and artwork.

In short

  • The app contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking code.
  • You do not sign up, and the app asks for no personal details.
  • Everything you track is stored in the app's own database on your device.
  • The app contacts TMDB for information and artwork about movies and shows, and YouTube for trailer thumbnails. Neither is sent your library.
  • Your ratings, watchlist and favorites leave the device only if you link your own TMDB account and choose to sync them. Your watch history is never sent anywhere.

On your device

The app stores the following in its private storage on the phone:

  • Your library: every movie and episode you mark watched, with the date and time you gave it; your ratings; your watchlist, favorites and dropped shows; and the formats and services you say you own a title on.
  • Your settings: the time zone and the "where to watch" country you chose.
  • Information about the titles you track, as received from TMDB: descriptions, air dates, cast and similar details, plus a cache of the images you have seen.
  • A TMDB link, if you make one: see the TMDB account section below.

The only device permission the app uses is network access. It reads no contacts, location, photos, microphone, camera or advertising identifier, and it opens a file only when you pick one yourself to restore a backup.

Android's own backup

If backup is switched on for your phone, Android includes the app's database, meaning your library and settings, in the backup it keeps in your Google account, and in a direct transfer to a new phone. That copy is made and held by Google under your Google account and Google's privacy policy; the developer of sofalog has no access to it. The secret that links a TMDB account is kept out of both. You can turn this off, or delete the backup, in your phone's and your Google account's backup settings.

Backup files you create

Under Settings you can save your library to a backup file, or share that file with another app. The file holds your watch history, ratings, watchlist, favorites, dropped shows and owned copies, and never your settings or a TMDB link. It goes only where you put it, and whoever holds the file can read it. Restoring reads the one file you pick, either a sofalog backup or a Ticket Box export, and nothing else on the device.

Outside services the app contacts

The app connects to the services below directly from your phone. Like any internet connection, each one sees your IP address and basic technical details of the request. None of them receives your library or your watch history.

  • TMDB (themoviedb.org) provides the information and artwork for movies and shows. The app sends it the search terms you type, the ids of the titles it fetches, and the filters you pick on the Discover page, which can include your "where to watch" country. These requests use an API key that belongs to the app, the same for every user, and carry nothing that identifies you. Posters and other images are loaded from TMDB's image servers. The "where to watch" listings are sourced by TMDB from JustWatch; the app never contacts JustWatch.
  • YouTube supplies the thumbnail images of trailers. No video plays inside the app; tapping a trailer opens YouTube, outside the app.

What these services keep about a request is governed by their own privacy policies: TMDB and Google, for YouTube and for Android's backup.

Your own TMDB account, only if you link it

Linking is optional and off until you start it under Settings. It happens on TMDB's own website, where you sign in and approve or refuse it; the app never sees your TMDB password. Once linked:

  • The app stores the session id TMDB issues, encrypted and excluded from every backup, together with your TMDB account id and username and your choice of what to keep in sync.
  • For each of ratings, watchlist and favorites that you choose to sync, the app sends your changes to your TMDB account. When you ask for it, it also reads those same lists from the account once, to import them.
  • Your watch history is never sent.
  • Unlinking deletes the link from the device, ends the session at TMDB and stops all of this. What was already written to your TMDB account stays there, under TMDB's own privacy policy.

Links that leave the app

Links to a title's page on TMDB or IMDb, to a trailer on YouTube and to the project on GitHub open in your browser or in that service's own app. From there on, that service's privacy policy applies.

Who can see your data

  • You, on your device.
  • The developer of sofalog: nothing. There is no telemetry and no call home of any kind.
  • Nobody else. Your data is not sold, shared or used for advertising. The only disclosures are the ones described on this page, each of which you control.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays on the device until you remove it. There is no copy for the developer to delete.

  • You can remove any single watch entry, rating, list entry or owned copy from its page.
  • Settings → Delete data removes either your whole library, or everything the app keeps: library, settings, TMDB link and downloaded images.
  • Clearing the app's storage in Android's settings, or uninstalling the app, removes everything it kept on the device.
  • A copy in Android's backup is deleted from your Google account's backup settings. Backup files you saved or shared are yours to delete where you put them.
  • Nothing here deletes what was synced to a linked TMDB account; that is removed on TMDB.

Security

Every connection the app makes is encrypted in transit (HTTPS). Your library sits in the app's private storage, which Android keeps other apps out of, and the TMDB session id is stored encrypted.

Children

sofalog is not directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect information from anyone.

Connecting to a sofalog server

A later version is planned to let you connect the app to a sofalog server that you, or someone in your household, runs. The app cannot do this today. This policy will be updated before that feature is released; what such a server holds is described in the privacy policy for the web app.

Changes to this policy
