Privacy policy

What sofalog does with your data

Effective 1 November 2026 · applies to the sofalog app for Android and the web app it opens

sofalog is a self-hosted media tracker. The app is a client for a sofalog server that you, or someone in your household, runs. The developer of sofalog operates no server for it, has no account system of its own and never receives, sees or stores anything you do in the app. Everything below describes where your data goes instead: your device, the server you connect to, and the outside services that server talks to on your behalf.

In short

  • The app contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or tracking code.
  • It sends data to one place: the sofalog server whose address you open it at.
  • That server is run by its administrator, not by the developer. They hold your account and your library, and this policy tells you what that is.
  • Your phone never talks to TMDB, YouTube, Jellyfin or Steam directly. The server does, and only for the purposes listed below.

On your device

The app keeps three things on the phone, and nothing else:

  • A session cookie, an opaque random value that keeps you signed in. It identifies your session on your server and carries no personal data itself.
  • A cache of the app and of pages you have viewed, including poster images, so that the app opens and shows what you last looked at while offline. It is cleared when you clear the app's data or uninstall it.
  • The server address you connect to.

The app asks for no device permissions beyond network access, and reads no contacts, location, files, photos or identifiers.

On the server you connect to

Everything you do in the app is stored on that server, in its database and on its disk. This is what it holds about you:

  • Your account: your email address, which is your sign-in identity; an optional display name; and either a password, stored only as an argon2id hash, or nothing when the server uses single sign-on. An administrator can also set a time zone and, for the games feature, a Steam id you give them.
  • Your sign-ins: for each session, when it was created, the browser or app identifier your device sent, and the IP address the connection came from. A session expires after 30 days without use, and logging out deletes it.
  • Request logs: like most servers, sofalog writes one log line per request with the path, the result, the time taken and the client IP address. Where those lines go and how long they are kept is up to the server's administrator.
  • Your library: every movie and episode you mark watched, with the date and time you gave it; your ratings; your watchlist, favorites and dropped shows; the formats and services you say you own an item on; your time zone; and whether you opted into matching watchlists.
  • A TMDB link, if you make one: the session id TMDB issues when you approve the link, your TMDB account id and username, and which of ratings, watchlist and favorites you chose to keep in sync. See the TMDB section below.

Who can see it

  • You. Your library is yours alone. Another user of the same server sees none of your history, ratings or lists.
  • The server's administrator manages the accounts on it and can see their email addresses and display names under Settings → Users. As the operator of the server they also hold the database, the artwork and the logs, and are the person to ask about how long anything is kept.
  • Other users, only if you opt in. The one sharing feature is matching watchlists: when you switch it on in your account settings, another user can see which titles are on both of your watchlists, and nothing else. It is off by default, and only you can turn it on.
  • The developer of sofalog: nothing. There is no telemetry, no update check and no call home of any kind.

Outside services the server talks to

The app itself contacts only your server. Your server contacts the services below, and each one sees only what is stated here. None of them receives your account, your library or your watch history, with the single exception of a TMDB account you choose to link.

  • TMDB (themoviedb.org) provides the metadata and artwork for movies and shows. The server sends it the search terms you type and the ids of the titles it fetches, using the administrator's API key. Posters and other images are downloaded by the server and served to you from there, so your device never loads anything from TMDB. The "where to watch" listings TMDB provides are sourced by TMDB from JustWatch; sofalog never contacts JustWatch.
  • Your own TMDB account, only if you link it. Linking happens on TMDB's own website, where you approve or refuse it; sofalog never sees your TMDB password. If you then turn on syncing, the server sends your ratings, your watchlist and your favorites to your TMDB account, and can read those same lists from it once, when you ask. Your watch history is never sent. Unlinking stops all of this and deletes the link from the server; what was already written to TMDB stays there, under TMDB's own privacy policy.
  • YouTube supplies the thumbnail images of trailers. The server fetches each image once and serves it to you itself; your device never contacts YouTube, and no video plays inside the app.
  • Jellyfin, if the administrator connected one, is their own media server. sofalog reads which movies and seasons it holds, so that the app can show them as available, and never what anyone has played.
  • A single sign-on provider, if the administrator configured one, handles your sign-in instead of a password. sofalog receives your verified email address and, when offered, your name, and nothing else. The provider's own privacy policy governs what it keeps.
  • Steam, once the games feature ships, will be read by the server with the Steam id you provide, to list your games, hours and achievements. Nothing is written to Steam.

Your choices and deleting your data

  • You can change your display name, time zone and password, turn matching watchlists on or off, and unlink your TMDB account at any time under Settings.
  • You can remove any single watch entry, rating, list entry or owned copy from its page.
  • Logging out deletes that session on the server. Uninstalling the app removes everything it kept on your device.
  • To delete your account, ask the server's administrator, who removes it under Settings → Users. Deleting an account deletes it together with every session, watch, rating, list entry, copy and TMDB link stored with it. Server logs that mention your IP address are kept or rotated according to the administrator's own practice.

Children

sofalog is not directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect information from anyone. Who may hold an account on a given server is the administrator's decision.

Changes to this policy

When this page changes, the date at the top changes with it. The full history of every revision is public in the project's source repository.

Contact

Questions about this policy or about the app go to the project's issue tracker on GitHub. Questions about a particular server, its accounts or its logs go to that server's administrator.
